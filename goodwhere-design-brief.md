# GOODWHERE / One

A proposed new consumer GPS device and brand for Care & Bloom.

## Recommendation
Launch GOODWHERE One: a palm-sized cellular GPS companion for an owner’s car, camper and valuable equipment. A new industrial design, a recognizable brand and an owned app experience, using a qualified partner platform underneath where feasible.

The product is not a reskin of the benchmark trackers. The enclosure, attachments, packaging and interaction language are new design work. These images are industrial-design concepts, not CAD, working prototypes or validated engineering specifications.

## Name and positioning
**Brand:** GOODWHERE, styled lowercase in the wordmark.
**Product:** One.
**Service:** Goodwhere Connect.
**Campaign:** Go on.
**Positioning:** Connected confidence for the things that make life yours.

“Good” carries reassurance; “where” carries the category without locking the brand to GPS jargon. It can extend to home, travel and outdoor products. The short product name keeps the longer brand usable: Goodwhere One. Pronounce it “good-where.”

Exact-name public web searches on September 16, 2026 (Pacific) did not surface an obvious consumer electronics brand in the returned results. This is a preliminary creative screen, not trademark, company-name, domain or handle clearance. No registrations or purchases have been made. Complete clearance in intended sales markets before adopting the name.

## Three industrial directions
A. Loop — soft rectangular porcelain body with a vermilion-lined attachment aperture. Warm, tactile and recognizable. Recommended direction following visual review of all three concepts.
B. Arc — organic moss-green pebble with a butter-yellow side accent. More outdoors-oriented; less immediately recognizable from the front.
C. Tab — graphite precision slab with a cobalt woven pull-tab. More equipment-oriented; fabric needs wear and cleaning validation.

## Proposed first product
One device, one app and two simple attachment choices. Start with owned vehicles and leisure equipment; do not pretend one device is simultaneously an optimized pet wearable, child-safety device and luggage product.

Proposed experience: on-demand location, movement alerts, safe-zone alerts, route history, shared access by invitation and a clear last-successful-update timestamp. Cellular connectivity, plan and coverage required. All features require partner confirmation and validation on the final product.

Physical design target for Loop: approximately 76 × 46 × 19 mm. This is a volume brief, not a committed specification. Microtextured RF-transparent polymer; warm porcelain launch finish with a vermilion aperture detail; moss and ink as later color options. Single restrained status light. Rear charging contacts with an aligned cradle are a design proposal; sealing, corrosion and thermal performance need engineering validation.

Accessory concepts: removable woven carry loop; mechanically retained vehicle cradle with a qualified attachment method. Magnetic retention alone is not assumed adequate for every vehicle location. Keep the RF antenna region clear of conductive obstructions and validate actual mounting locations.

Do not publish a battery-life, IP, temperature or location-accuracy rating until tested. Battery volume, antenna keep-outs, reporting cadence and cellular coverage determine the feasible final envelope. Do not carry over another enclosure’s certifications. Locosafe’s 10,000 mAh Orb battery claims cannot be transferred into this proposed smaller device.

## Brand system
Porcelain #F0EDE4; poppy #F04C32; moss #55674F; ink #242B2B; butter #E9D78A. Warm, matte, tactile. Restrained lowercase typography. Large confident type in campaigns, sparse packaging, lifestyle photography about ownership and freedom. The orange-lined aperture is the proposed physical signature.

Lead with “Go on.” Show the owner’s cherished car, their weekend camper, their useful gear. Prove the service with real app behavior and honest coverage/last-update information. Avoid fear-based surveillance imagery and promises of covert or undetectable tracking.

## Commercial direction
The existing pitch’s approximately $200 annual subscription is a business-model scenario, not a product fact. A new enclosure adds engineering, tooling and certification costs that require supplier quotes and a revised contribution model. Confirm willingness to pay and full first-year outlay before fixing the offer.

## Development sequence
1. Resolve partner architecture: modem, antenna, PCB, cell, firmware, coverage and the ownership of tooling/data.
2. Commission actual industrial design CAD and volume models; prove the antenna and battery envelope before freezing the exterior.
3. Engineering prototypes, field tests and design validation. Check mounting, drop resistance, thermal behavior, charging, ingress and real battery modes.
4. Certification review, tooling and production validation, then a bounded consumer pilot. The earlier 90-day acquisition plan is a validation program, not a promise that a new hardware product ships in 90 days.
